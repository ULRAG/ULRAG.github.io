The EU’s Emissions Trading Scheme: Pros, Cons, and U.S. Viability

By: Joshua Walker  |  Date: February 11, 2022

The EU introduced the Emissions Trading Scheme (ETS) in 2005 to cap industrial CO₂ emissions by creating tradable carbon credits. While effective in reducing emissions, the system imposes disproportionate transaction costs on small firms.

Despite reductions in European emissions (3.8% between 2008 and 2016), the U.S. has not adopted a similar program. Reasons include cultural individualism and structural election differences, such as the U.S.’s single-member district system vs. Europe's multi-member districts.

Though imperfect, the ETS showcases how policy, political culture, and economic structures intersect in climate change strategies. The U.S. may remain slow in adopting similar systems due to inefficiencies, labor concerns, and political gridlock.
