Why It Would Be a Mistake to Overturn New York Times Co. v. Sullivan: A Discussion of the Landmark Case and its CriticismsNew York Times Co. v. Sullivan

By Caroline Kostuch — February 11, 2022

The Supreme Court’s landmark decision in New York Times Co. v. Sullivan (1964) reshaped American libel law by requiring public officials to prove “actual malice” before recovering damages for defamation. This standard strengthened constitutional protections for the press, enabling vigorous reporting on government and public figures without fear of retaliation.

This article examines growing calls to overturn or limit Sullivan, critiques arising from its expansion to cover all public figures, and evaluates whether weakening these protections would endanger democratic accountability. The author argues that preserving Sullivan is essential to safeguarding press freedom and maintaining its role as a check on governmental power.
